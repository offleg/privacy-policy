Privacy Policy

This privacy policy applies to the Foodprints app (hereby referred to as "Application") for mobile devices that was created by OffLeg (hereby referred to as "Service Provider") as a Free service. This service is intended for use "AS IS".


Information Collection and Use

The Application collects information when you download and use it. This information may include information such as

  • Your device's Internet Protocol address (e.g. IP address)
  • The pages of the Application that you visit, the time and date of your visit, the time spent on those pages
  • The time spent on the Application
  • The operating system you use on your mobile device


The Application does not gather precise information about the location of your mobile device.


What the Application stores. To keep your food memories safe and available on every device you sign in on, the Application stores what you enter: your meals, dishes, restaurant names and locations you type, ratings, notes, tags, prices, the dish photos you add, and the name, city and profile picture you choose to add to your profile. If you answer one of the Application's short in-app questions (for example "What would make you use Foodprints more often?"), your answer and anything you type with it are stored too. This information is kept on your device and on the Service Provider's servers (hosted by Supabase), linked to your account. Dish photos and profile pictures are stored privately and are not public.


From your photos. If you choose photos with "From your photos", the Application reads only the date each photo was taken, on your device, to remind you when you ate. It never keeps or sends where a photo was taken: it keeps a smaller copy of each photo with all such hidden details removed. The copies stay on your device until you save one as a dish (it is then stored like any other dish photo) or remove it.


Sharing your food memories. You can choose to let other Foodprints users see some of your food memories: all of them, your favourites, or dishes you pick. Nothing is shared unless you create a share and send its private link (or the 8-character code in it), and nothing is ever made public. Each link works for up to 5 people and for 7 days.

  • What the people you invite see: your first and last name from your profile, and for each shared dish its name, rating, whether it is a favourite or you would order it again, the restaurant name, the date and its photos. Your notes are shown only if you switch them on for that share. Prices, bill photos and restaurant locations are never shown. If both names on your profile are blank, your email address or phone number is shown in place of a name.
  • What you see: the names of the people who joined your share, shown the same way.
  • They can only look. They cannot change your memories, and nothing is copied into their own diary. The Application shows shared photos without saving them on their device, but it cannot stop someone from taking a screenshot.
  • You stay in control. You can remove a person or stop sharing at any time, and their access ends straight away. Turning a link off stops anyone new from joining with it. Anyone can also remove someone else's share from their own list.
  • What the Service Provider stores for sharing: each share and what it includes; its links, with the code kept only in scrambled form, when each link ends and how many people used it; who is in each share and who was removed; and how many wrong codes each account entered per day, to stop people guessing codes. Simple counts of sharing actions (for example "a share was created") are recorded with no names, codes or dishes.
  • The invitation page a link opens (offleg.com/foodprints/join) shows no names, sends nothing to the Service Provider and uses no tracking.

Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI). Two optional features use Google's Gemini AI, through the Service Provider's own servers:

  • Bill scanning: when you scan a restaurant bill, the photo of the bill is sent to Google Gemini to read the dish names, prices and restaurant. The Service Provider does not keep the bill photo after it has been read.
  • Voice quick add: your phone's built-in speech recognition (provided by Apple or Google) turns what you say into text. Before that text is sent to Google Gemini to understand which dishes you mentioned, the Application removes phone numbers, email addresses, card numbers and Aadhaar numbers from it. Your voice recording itself is not sent to the Service Provider.

Google processes this information under its own terms and privacy policy, available at https://policies.google.com/privacy. These features are used only when you choose them, and the information sent is not used by the Service Provider for advertising.


Permissions. The Application asks for access to your camera (to scan bills and photograph dishes), your photos (to add existing photos, use "From your photos" and choose a profile picture), and your microphone and speech recognition (for voice quick add). Each is asked for only when you first use that feature, and you can refuse or withdraw it in your device settings; the rest of the Application keeps working.

The Application uses notifications for reminders to save what you ate (on Friday, Saturday and Sunday evenings, and on the evening after you sign up) and for an occasional one-question survey. Reminders are on by default, and you can turn them off at any time in the Application's Settings or in your device settings. On iPhone, the Application starts them quietly, without asking: they go to your notification list without sound or banner, and Settings offers "Allow alerts" if you want full notifications. On Android 13 and newer, your phone asks you once whether to allow notifications. Reminders are scheduled on your device and are not sent from the Service Provider's servers.

Meta's handling of that information is governed by its own privacy policy, available at https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy/. Staff Access
